# Pronunciation sources

Verified 2026-10-18. These are English pronunciations of botanical terms and
scientific family names, using broad American English IPA. They are not claims
about reconstructed Latin pronunciation or the only accepted way to say a name.

Dictionary.com and Cambridge publish IPA. Merriam-Webster uses its own notation;
its entries below were manually transcribed to IPA after checking each entry.
Ray Angelo's *Pronunciation of Plant Names of New England Genera and Families*
(revised March 2022, hosted by the New England Botanical Society) uses respelling.
Its Asparagaceae and Crassulaceae entries were checked through indexed PDF text;
the PDF download URL returned 404 during verification.

## Notation

Spaces and syllable dots in source transcriptions are omitted. English /r/ is
used conventionally rather than narrow [ɹ]; /ɚ/ represents unstressed /ər/ and
/ɝ/ stressed /ər/. Sources differ in whether American /i/ and /u/ include length
marks. Both /ɛr/ and /ɛər/ occur in broad American transcriptions. These notation
differences do not indicate different intended pronunciations. Ovary uses the
source's /ˈoʊvəri/ spelling to avoid obscuring its syllable structure.

For multiword labels, the component dictionary pronunciations are combined;
root and bud were separately checked at [Dictionary.com root](https://www.dictionary.com/browse/root)
and [Dictionary.com bud](https://www.dictionary.com/browse/bud). Petal uses broad
/t/ rather than a narrow American flap transcription; an unstressed schwa is
shown before its final /l/.
